Decisions

Live price changes

How it works, and the safety rules
  • When it decides: within about 2 minutes of any Amazon offer change on one of your listings (at most once per listing every 2 minutes).
  • Prices it considers: your current price, tie the lowest rival, 1¢ under it; when Amazon's target price for you is fresh (read in the last 45 min) also the target and ±1–2% steps. Always inside the SKU's Seller Snap min and max.
  • Buy Box chance comes from a model trained nightly on the last 7 days of your own data (your price vs the target and vs the lowest rival, number of rivals, your share of stock).
  • Undercuts account for how often that rival follows (learned per rival) and a price-war penalty: how far the listing's lowest price typically falls in the next 24 h.
  • Value = units it expects to sell × (net proceeds − what the unit is worth if kept and sold later near today's low, less the cost of waiting). What you paid is treated as sunk, so below-cost liquidation listings still sell.
  • Live only when all three are true: the Render setting LIVE_MODE=on, "Change prices" ticked here, and the SKU is a live SKU with Seller Snap repricing off.
  • Every live change: within min/max, no bigger than the step limit, not sooner than the minutes between changes, within the daily cap; logged below. "Stop live pricing" switches it off at once.
  • Buy Box suppressed (orange S on the row): Amazon shows no Buy Box on the listing — every offer is above the price Amazon sees at other retailers. The repricer then doesn't chase Amazon's target price; it matches the lowest offer on the listing (any fulfillment, price + shipping), within your min and max, and the step limit and brakes work from there. It spots suppression from Amazon's offer-change message (no Buy Box price and no winner). Suppressed moments are left out of the Buy Box model's training.
  • Brakes on runs of cuts: if a rival answers your cuts (moves to or below your new price within the window) the set number of times in a row, cuts on that SKU pause for the set hours and the SKU is listed under Live pricing. A cut also never takes the price more than the max drop below its highest point in the last 24 hours. Raises aren't braked: they're bounded by your max and correct themselves on the next offer change.

Models, algorithms and logic

1. Buy Box model: "what's my chance of holding the Buy Box at price p?"
  • Type: logistic regression (a weighted sum of inputs passed through an S-curve, giving a probability between 0 and 1). Two versions: one used when Amazon's target price for you is fresh (read in the last 45 min), one used without it.
  • Inputs (all built the same way in training and in live decisions):
    • your price vs the lowest rival FBA offer, in %, split into "below" (capped at 1% below; going deeper buys little) and "above";
    • tied with the lowest rival (within 0.5%), and more than 1% above it;
    • number of rival FBA offers (logarithm);
    • your share of the competitive stock (your fulfillable units ÷ yours + rivals' within 2% of the low, from Keepa), with a flag when rival stock isn't known;
    • with a target: your price vs Amazon's target in % ("below", capped at 1%, and "above"), and whether you're at or below it.
  • Training data: every target-price reading from the last 7 days, matched to the offers on the listing at that moment. The answer it learns is your actual Buy Box share over the next 30 minutes (a fraction, not just yes/no).
  • Fitting: maximum likelihood with mild regularisation (scikit-learn, L2, C = 1). The last 2 days are held out first to measure accuracy, then it's refit on all 7 days.
  • Accuracy is shown above as AUC (how well it ranks winning vs losing moments: 0.5 = coin flip, 1.0 = perfect) and Brier score (average squared error of the probabilities; lower is better), on the held-out days.
2. Rival reaction model: "if I undercut, will they follow, and how fast?"
  • Events: every time any seller cut below every other FBA offer, each rival that was at the low just before is checked: did it match or go below within 60 minutes?
  • Per-rival rate (empirical Bayes): a rival that reacted k times out of n gets rate = (k + a) ÷ (n + a + b). The prior a, b is fitted to all rivals with 5+ events (method of moments on a Beta distribution), so a rival with little history starts near the average and moves toward its own record as events pile up.
  • Lag: each rival's median minutes to react (3+ reactions), otherwise the pooled median.
  • Price-war table: for undercuts with 24 h of data after them, the median % drop in the listing's lowest price a day later, grouped by how reactive the undercut rivals are; values in between are interpolated.
3. Demand and fees
  • Sales per Buy Box hour = Seller Snap 30-day units/day ÷ 24 ÷ your Seller Snap Buy Box share (floored at 25%, so it never assumes more than 4× today's sales if you won every Buy Box).
  • Units in the next 24 h at price p = sales per Buy Box hour × 24 × Buy Box chance(p), never more than your stock.
  • Fees(p) come from Amazon's own fee estimate for the SKU (Product Fees API), pulled daily at a price under $10, $10–$50 and over $50, so the steps are exact: referral 8% at $10.00 or less and 15% above (for beauty), and the FBA fee's lower rate under $10 and higher rate over $50. Until a SKU's fees are loaded it uses 8%/15% plus a typical FBA fee, and says so in "Why".
  • Profit per unit = price − Amazon fees − cost − $1.40 prep; ROI = profit ÷ cost, the way Seller Snap figures it. Seller Snap's own ROI column is the same formula at your average sale price over about the last week, not at today's price.
  • Not yet modelled: how much demand itself changes with price (needs small live price tests).
4. Value of each candidate price (the decision)
days to sell   = your stock ÷ today's expected daily sales            (max 180)
value if kept  = (lowest rival − fees at that price) × (1 − cost of waiting × days to sell ÷ 2)
cost of waiting = profit per day ÷ money in inventory (cost + prep), across the store, when the switch is on
value(p)       = units(p) × [(p − fees(p)) − value if kept] − price-war penalty(p)

if p is a new cut below every rival:
  Buy Box chance = (1 − r) × chance alone + r × [f × chance alone + (1 − f) × chance tied at p]
                   r = most reactive rival's rate at the low,  f = its lag ÷ 24 h
  penalty        = price-war drop(r) × lowest rival × (1 − referral) × min(stock, 30 days of sales)
  • What you paid (cost and prep) is treated as sunk: a unit is worth its net proceeds if sold now, or the discounted value of selling it later if kept. That's why below-cost liquidation stock still sells, and why it's patient when stock will sell through soon anyway.
  • Cost of waiting: a sale pushed back a day costs about what that cash would have earned back in inventory for a day. With the switch on it's your store's own figure (profit a day ÷ money in inventory), updated hourly. Gain/day is the value difference; Profit/day shows the plain 24-hour profit at each price, so you can see when it gives up sales today for a better price.
  • Choice: the candidate with the highest value. It recommends a change only if that beats the current price by at least the minimum gain ($ per day and % — see "How it decides") and by 1¢ or more; otherwise Hold.
  • Labels: Raise to target, Raise to next offer (1¢ under the next rival when you're alone at the low), Raise, Lower to target, Match lowest, Undercut.
  • Without a fresh target price only three moves are considered (tie, 1¢ under the lowest rival, next offer − 1¢), because the model without a target is much weaker.
5. Retraining and checks
  • Retrained nightly at 3:17 AM Central on the last 7 days (GitHub Actions in the offer-recorder repo) and uploaded to the recorder; the Model card shows when.
  • Scorecard (tools/evaluate.py): predicted vs actual Buy Box share, the action mix, and how often Seller Snap ended up at the recommended price anyway. Run before any live test.
