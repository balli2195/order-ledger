Recovery

lost at your sourcing cost vs what Amazon paid back

Lost inbound = units missing from closed shipment plans (month the plan closed). Lost / damaged in warehouse = the Profit page's write-offs from the inventory ledger (lost is net of units found). Paid = Amazon's Lost_Inbound, Lost_Warehouse and Damaged_Warehouse reimbursements by approval month, net of clawbacks. Shortfall = lost at your cost − paid. Damaged units Amazon moves to “distributor damaged” (it says they arrived damaged) are shown separately as Not Amazon’s fault and left out of the shortfall. “By month paid” dates payments by approval; “by month of loss” puts each payment in the month of the loss it pays for (warehouse: first-in-first-out by SKU; lost inbound: the month the case’s shipment plan closed), so recent months still fill in as Amazon pays. Refreshes daily with the Hub's Amazon sync.

How the money came back (Amazon paid, by month)

Amount disputes top up payouts already made, and approved sourcing-cost raises make later payouts bigger; neither adds to "lost". Their effect shows as a higher payout rate (paid ÷ those units at your cost).
